About

I am an ScM student in Biostatistics at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health. My research interests center on developing valid, privacy-preserving, and transportable methods for generating causal evidence from fragmented clinical and real-world data.

I work with Dr. Jiayi (Jessie) Tong on survival-data reconstruction and multi-site statistical learning. I also support tuberculosis screening research at the Johns Hopkins School of Medicine. Previously, I worked on wearable and multimodal disease data at Southern University of Science and Technology and served as a United Nations Volunteer with UNFPA in Ecuador.

Research interests: survival analysis · causal inference · federated learning · data integration · clinical trials · evidence synthesis.

Research

Kaplan–Meier reconstruction & evidence synthesis

October 2025–present · Advisor: Dr. Jiayi Tong

I benchmark methods for reconstructing individual patient data from published Kaplan–Meier curves, examining how reconstruction affects survival estimates and downstream meta-analysis. My simulation study considers proportional hazards, delayed effects, crossing curves, censoring, and digitization error, with evaluation of hazard ratios and restricted mean survival time.

Manuscript in preparation.

Federated dictionary learning

August 2026–present · Advisor: Dr. Jiayi Tong

I am investigating shared dictionary representations for clinical prediction and phenotyping across hospitals, with patient-specific sparse coefficients retained locally. The work explores outcome-informed updates and aggregation of local summary statistics to balance representation quality and communication.

CHASE-TB: tuberculosis screening trial

December 2025–present · Johns Hopkins School of Medicine
Supervisors: Dr. Emily Kendall & Dr. Joowhan Sung

As a part-time research assistant, I support record linkage, data management, and statistical analysis for a multiple-period crossover trial of facility- and community-based TB screening in Uganda. My work connects screening records with routine health records and treatment registers, using reproducible R workflows to investigate diagnostic outcomes and linkage to care.

Wearable & multimodal disease data

August 2024–June 2025 · Southern University of Science and Technology
Supervisor: Prof. Jian Qing Shi

As a full-time research assistant, I integrated wearable gait signals, EEG, cognitive assessments, and clinical information for neurodegenerative-disease screening research. I applied mixture models and semi-supervised learning to study patient heterogeneity and implemented feature extraction and comparative classification analyses.
